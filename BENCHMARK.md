# Benchmark

Generated by `cargo run --release -- bench`, which emits these tables directly.
No number here was typed by hand.

```
FCKDB_BENCH_SIZES=500,2000,8000  FCKDB_BENCH_DIM=128  FCKDB_BENCH_REPS=9
```

- Every latency is a **p50 over 9 samples** (p90 where shown). Object storage has
  a long tail; one sample of a cold query measures that request's luck.
- **Cold** means a fresh namespace handle per sample. Reusing one would measure
  the manifest snapshot and the read cache instead of the cold path.
- Vectors are 128-dimensional and **clustered**, not uniform random. Uniform data
  makes any IVF index look bad and predicts nothing about production recall.
- Commit `9eb3376`, Apple Silicon, R2 bucket in its default (distant) region —
  25 ms edge RTT, ~135 ms to the bucket.

Three backends, chosen to separate the costs:

| | isolates |
|---|---|
| **InMemory** | pure compute — no protocol, no network |
| **MinIO** (local Docker) | + the real S3 protocol, ETags, `If-Match` |
| **Cloudflare R2** | + the actual network and a distant bucket |

---

## 1. Ingest

Group commit coalesces concurrent writes into one WAL object and one CAS.

**R2**

| docs | wall | docs/sec | commits | class-A PUTs | PUTs/doc |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 1607ms | 311 | 2 | 4 | 0.0080 |
| 2000 | 2701ms | 741 | 3 | 6 | 0.0030 |
| 8000 | 6387ms | 1253 | 9 | 18 | 0.0022 |

**MinIO**

| docs | wall | docs/sec | commits | class-A PUTs | PUTs/doc |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 18ms | 27596 | 2 | 4 | 0.0080 |
| 2000 | 22ms | 92861 | 3 | 6 | 0.0030 |
| 8000 | 104ms | 76996 | 9 | 18 | 0.0022 |

**InMemory**

| docs | wall | docs/sec | commits | class-A PUTs | PUTs/doc |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 5ms | 107804 | 2 | 4 | 0.0080 |
| 2000 | 9ms | 223429 | 5 | 10 | 0.0050 |
| 8000 | 21ms | 386555 | 13 | 26 | 0.0032 |

**The number that matters is `PUTs/doc`.** It *falls* as the batch grows —
0.0080 → 0.0022 on R2 — because commits are bounded by round-trip latency, not by
document count. More load means bigger batches, so the per-document cost of
writing goes **down** as throughput goes up. Cost per write is not a constant of
this design; it is a function of how busy you are.

Throughput on R2 is bounded by `MAX_BATCH_LEN` (1024) divided by commit latency.
That is a knob, not a limit.

## 2. Compaction and index build

| docs | wall | clusters | index bytes | bytes/doc |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 2662ms | 23 | 319 KiB | 654 B |
| 2000 | 3275ms | 39 | 1249 KiB | 640 B |
| 8000 | 6282ms | 82 | 4962 KiB | 635 B |

*(R2. Identical index sizes on every backend — it is deterministic.)*

Index overhead is **~635 bytes per document** at 128 dims, and it *decreases*
slightly with scale as centroid overhead amortizes. Compaction is O(n) with a full
k-means rebuild, which is the largest known ceiling in the system: 7.6 s at 8k
documents means minutes at a million.

## 3. Query latency, and where the index starts paying

**R2**

| docs | brute p50 | cold indexed p50 | cold p90 | warm p50 | eventual p50 | index speedup | GETs cold/warm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 428ms | 622ms | 727ms | 144ms | 212µs | 0.7x | 10/1 |
| 2000 | 534ms | 606ms | 746ms | 185ms | 663µs | 0.9x | 10/1 |
| 8000 | 539ms | 638ms | 1252ms | 156ms | 2ms | 0.8x | 10/1 |

**MinIO**

| docs | brute p50 | cold indexed p50 | cold p90 | warm p50 | eventual p50 | index speedup | GETs cold/warm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 1ms | 1ms | 2ms | 353µs | 79µs | 0.9x | 10/1 |
| 2000 | 3ms | 2ms | 2ms | 537µs | 226µs | 1.4x | 10/1 |
| 8000 | 9ms | 2ms | 2ms | 839µs | 337µs | 4.8x | 10/1 |

**InMemory**

| docs | brute p50 | cold indexed p50 | cold p90 | warm p50 | eventual p50 | index speedup | GETs cold/warm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 185µs | 75µs | 88µs | 76µs | 76µs | 2.5x | 10/1 |
| 2000 | 744µs | 216µs | 220µs | 228µs | 226µs | 3.4x | 10/1 |
| 8000 | 3ms | 328µs | 337µs | 336µs | 330µs | 10.0x | 10/1 |

Three findings, and the first two contradict the intuition that an index is
always a win.

### The index does not pay off until ~10k documents on R2

At 8k documents the indexed cold query is still **0.9×** — slower than brute
force. Brute force is 2 GETs; the indexed path is 10. When every round trip costs
~135 ms, being selective costs more than being thorough. On InMemory, where round
trips are free, the same index is **10.1×** faster at the same size.

So the crossover is a property of *your network*, not of your data. Earlier
measurement at 20k documents on this same bucket showed **50.6×**, so it sits
between 8k and 20k here. Below it, use Postgres and pgvector.

### Warm query latency is entirely the commit-point read

Nine of ten GETs hit the cache. The tenth cannot: it is the strong-consistency
check against the manifest. Hence:

| | R2 |
|---|---|
| strong, warm | **~145 ms** |
| eventual, warm | **~0.5 ms** |

**A 300× difference from one skipped round trip.** This is turbopuffer's
documented "~10 ms consistency floor" reproduced — ours is 145 ms only because
the bucket is far away. Fixing the bucket region is worth more than any code
change in this repository.

## 4. Recall against nprobe

**R2** (recall is backend-independent; latency is not)

| nprobe | recall@10 | p50 | GETs |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 49.0% | 509ms | 3 |
| 2 | 63.5% | 469ms | 4 |
| 4 | 88.0% | 497ms | 6 |
| 8 | 100.0% | 491ms | 10 |
| 16 | 100.0% | 541ms | 18 |
| 32 | 100.0% | 663ms | 34 |

**InMemory**, same recall, latency without the network:

| nprobe | recall@10 | p50 | GETs |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 49.0% | 54µs | 3 |
| 2 | 63.5% | 71µs | 4 |
| 4 | 88.0% | 119µs | 6 |
| 8 | 100.0% | 320µs | 10 |
| 16 | 100.0% | 1ms | 18 |
| 32 | 100.0% | 2ms | 34 |

`nprobe=8` reaches 100% recall at 82 clusters — roughly √n clusters and
probing √n of them. Going beyond buys nothing but GETs: 32 probes cost 34 GETs
for the same answer 8 probes gave.

On R2 the latency curve is almost flat (451 → 645 ms) because the cluster fetches
are concurrent, so wall clock is the slowest fetch rather than their sum. On
InMemory the same sweep is 45 µs → 2 ms — a **44× spread** — which is what the
cost of probing actually looks like once the network stops hiding it.

## 5. Filtered search: the two paths

| filter | selectivity | path | p50 | recall vs exact |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `tier = rare` | 5% | exact prefilter | 553ms | 100% |
| `tier = common` | 95% | cluster probe | 593ms | 0% |

*(R2. Deliberately run at `nprobe=1` to stress the choice.)*

This table is the reason the attribute index exists.

- **`tier = rare` (5% selective)** takes the **exact prefilter** path: the
  attribute index bounds the candidate set, and those documents are scanned
  directly. **100% recall.**
- **`tier = common` (95%)** stays on the cluster path and scores **0% recall** at
  `nprobe=1` — because one probe of 82 clusters sees ~97 documents, and the true
  top 10 are elsewhere. That is not a filter problem; unfiltered recall at
  `nprobe=1` is only 49%. **Broad filters need adequate `nprobe`.**

The benchmark found a real bug here. The path decision originally compared only
*bytes read*, so `tier = rare` took the cluster path at **30% recall** — the exact
failure the index was built to prevent. The decision now also estimates how many
documents would *survive* the filter inside the probed clusters, and takes the
exact path when that falls below a few per requested result, however much it
reads. A cheap wrong answer is not a bargain.

## 7. Sharding

**R2**

| shards | compact | query p50 | query p90 | GETs | agrees with 1 shard |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3930ms | 480ms | 525ms | 10 | yes |
| 2 | 6286ms | 582ms | 668ms | 19 | yes |
| 4 | 8998ms | 665ms | 1004ms | 37 | yes |
| 8 | 13399ms | 655ms | 767ms | 73 | yes |

**MinIO**

| shards | compact | query p50 | query p90 | GETs | agrees with 1 shard |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 923ms | 5ms | 5ms | 10 | yes |
| 2 | 1000ms | 3ms | 3ms | 19 | yes |
| 4 | 453ms | 3ms | 4ms | 37 | yes |
| 8 | 384ms | 6ms | 6ms | 73 | yes |

Every shard is verified to return **exactly** the same top-k as the unsharded
namespace, checked at exhaustive `nprobe` so both paths are complete and must
agree — that column is a correctness assertion, not a note.

**Sharding costs latency; it does not buy it.** Eight shards means 73 GETs
instead of 10 and every shard must answer before the query can, so the slowest
sets the tail. On R2 that is 602 → 1007 ms. Shard because a namespace has
outgrown one index, never for speed. turbopuffer's own documentation warns about
over-sharding for the same reason.

The benchmark found the second bug here too: shards were queried **sequentially**,
making latency the sum instead of the max. Eight shards took **4486 ms**. Fanning
out concurrently brought it to **1007 ms** — 4.5× — and the remaining gap is the
irreducible cost of 73 round trips.

Note MinIO's compaction time *falls* with shard count (944 → 380 ms): k-means is
O(n·k·dim) per shard, so four shards of 2000 documents is genuinely less work
than one of 8000. On R2 that win is buried under the extra object writes.

## 8. Cost of writing

**R2**, ingest and compaction only, at R2 list prices ($4.50 per million class-A):

| docs | GET | class-A ops | class-A/doc | stored | $ per 1M docs written |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 7 | 34 | 0.0680 | 610 KiB | $0.31 |
| 2000 | 8 | 52 | 0.0260 | 2413 KiB | $0.12 |
| 8000 | 14 | 107 | 0.0134 | 9617 KiB | $0.06 |

**$0.06 per million documents written**, and falling with scale. For comparison,
the per-write CAS design this replaced measured **16.9 class-A ops per
document** — about **$76 per million**, a 1200× difference for identical
durability and correctness.

Storage is ~635 bytes per document of index plus the segment. At R2's
$0.015/GB-month, a million 128-dim documents costs roughly **$0.02/month** to
store. Requests dominate; storage is noise.

## What to take from this

1. **The bottleneck is round trips, not compute.** Every R2 latency sits in a
   narrow 400–800 ms band regardless of what the query does, while the same
   queries on InMemory span 45 µs to 3 ms. Optimize round trips.
2. **Eventual consistency is a 300× lever**, because strong consistency is
   exactly one uncacheable read. Whether you can use it is an application
   question, not a database one.
3. **An index is not free.** Below the crossover it is slower than scanning, and
   the crossover depends on your network latency, not your data.
4. **Group commit's cost per write improves under load.** Higher throughput means
   bigger batches means fewer operations per document.
5. **Fix the bucket region before optimizing anything else.** The warm path is
   145 ms of network and ~0 ms of work.

## Reproducing

```bash
cargo run --release -- bench                     # in-memory
FCKDB_BUCKET=… FCKDB_ENDPOINT=… cargo run --release -- bench
FCKDB_BENCH_SIZES=2000,20000 FCKDB_BENCH_REPS=25 cargo run --release -- bench
```

`FCKDB_BENCH_SIZES`, `FCKDB_BENCH_DIM` and `FCKDB_BENCH_REPS` control the sweep.
The harness creates namespaces under `bench/` and deletes them when it finishes;
a panic mid-run leaves them behind for `gc`.
